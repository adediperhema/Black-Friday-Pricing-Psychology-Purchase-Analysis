# **Black Friday Pricing Psychology \& Purchase Analysis**





## Project Overview

Shoppers spent **$35.1M** across 100,000 transactions on goods with a list value of $47.9M, so the retailer gave away **$12.7M** (26.6%) in discounts. The central finding is that **discount depth did not change how much people bought.** Units per transaction sit near 1.45 whether the discount is 5% or 60%, while average order value falls from $448 to $193. Deeper discounts simply reduced revenue per order irrespective of the **charm-pricing structure**.

Units per transaction are flat from items under $25 (1.43) to items of $1,000 and above (1.45), and the average discount is the same in every tier (26.6-26.8%). ## Purchase behaviour and recommendations



<img width="2000" height="1000" alt="dashboard" src="https://github.com/adediperhema/Black-Friday-Pricing-Psychology-Purchase-Analysis/blob/main/Black%20Friday%20Chart%20Image%203.png" />










• Customers: 82.5% of customers bought more than once in the 8 days (5,763 bought once), 34% bought four or more times, and the top 10% of customers account for 35.8% of spend. Customers whose average discount is 40%+ (2,299 people) averaged only 1.9 purchases and $510, though a small number of purchases makes any average more extreme.

• Timing: Black Friday (12,633 orders) looks like any other day: same average discount (26.6%), order value $347 vs $352. Daily revenue stays within $4.31M-$4.51M and hourly volume within 4,030-4,300 orders, so there is no demand peak to exploit.

• Demographics: age, gender and city show minimal difference in order value or discount. Category is the only meaningful driver of spend.








### Recommendations

1	Cap discounts at 40%	Only 8.0% of orders (8,014) exceed 40%. Capping them lifts revenue by about $475K if no sales are lost, $248K if 10% are lost; break-even is a 21% loss of those orders (What\_If sheet). Margin gain would be larger.

2	A/B test discount depth	This data has buyers only. Test 20% vs 30% vs 40% on matched traffic and measure conversion, not just basket size.

3	Tier discounts by price and category	Today a $20 book and a $2,000 TV get the same expected discount. Concentrate depth where margin allows; Electronics drives 40% of revenue.

4	Make loyalty visible	VIP and Loyal customers get the same discount as New ones (26.5% vs 26.7%). Use early access or perks instead of deeper blanket discounts.

5	Design the final price	Prices are formula-driven. Test .99/.90 endings after discount and a $99 vs $100 threshold before assuming charm pricing helps or not.
